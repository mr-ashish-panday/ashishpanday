# Portfolio Story — Minimal Personal Page Copywriting System

## Why This Exists

Generic "about page" frameworks produce generic output. This skill is built on
three specific bodies of knowledge:
1. **Narrative neuroscience** — what actually happens in the reader's brain
2. **Exemplar analysis** — Omar Waseem, Paul Graham, Derek Sivers, Naval
3. **Anti-patterns** — the six most common mistakes that kill personal pages

---

## The One Rule That Overrides All Others

> **A story makes the reader feel something. A resume makes the reader note something.**

Paul Zak's research: only emotionally tense narratives trigger oxytocin (empathy)
and cortisol (attention). A flat list of achievements triggers neither.
The brain processes story "as if it were happening to the reader."
Fact-lists are processed as information. Stories are processed as experience.

**Implication**: Every paragraph must have either tension, specificity, or both.
If a paragraph has neither, cut it.

---

## The Five Principles (In Priority Order)

### 1. Tension First
Every great personal page has a moment of stakes. Not drama — stakes.
- "I started my first company at 12" (stakes: young, audacious)
- "to learn how research truly happens" (stakes: admission of gap, intellectual
  honesty)
- "Can't say more — strong NDA" (stakes: you're in rooms most people aren't)

Tension can be subtle. It doesn't need to be a sob story.
Ask for each paragraph: *what is at stake here or what is the reader curious about?*

### 2. Specificity Is Credibility
Vague = worthless. Specific = instant trust.

| Kill This | Use This |
|---|---|
| "worked with big creators" | "Carlos (5B+, YT Shorts), Logan (7B+, TikTok)" |
| "large YouTube launch" | "one of the largest business series launches of 2025" |
| "I enjoy research" | "Currently working on something I think will change how we think about context windows" |
| "built internal systems" | "helped Blastly (Canada) build their internal systems" |

Brain science basis: unique, specific details are the "hooks" that improve memory
retention. Non-specific claims leave no memory trace.

### 3. Voice Above All
The single most common mistake: writing in "professional voice" instead of your
actual voice. Sylvie DiGiusto: "the worst mistake is trying to sound like someone else."

Markers of authentic voice in great founder pages:
- Short declarative sentences ("Good old days.")
- Comfortable with incompleteness ("Can't say more.")
- Admits what they don't know ("to learn how research truly happens")
- Has a point of view on their own work

Paul Graham principle: "The less energy readers expend on your prose, the more
they'll have left for your ideas." Plain English. No jargon. No buzzwords.

### 4. The Red Thread (Unifying Diverse Backgrounds)
When background spans multiple fields (research, enterprise, creator economy, tech),
find the ONE consistent drive underneath them all.

Process:
1. List every major thing you've done
2. Ask: "What motivated me to do each of these?"
3. The answer that comes up in 3+ cases is your red thread

The red thread doesn't need to be stated explicitly. It should be *felt* through
how connections are drawn between experiences.

Example for Ashish:
- Research (LLMs, context windows) → obsession with how intelligence handles
  information
- Creator economy backend (billions of views) → obsession with how systems scale
- GTM system (surfacing executives from data) → same obsession applied to humans
- RemoteArmz → the synthesis

The thread: **"systems that handle information at scale, whether artificial or human."**

### 5. Minimalism = Every Sentence Earns Its Place
Paul Graham: "Cut any word that doesn't add meaning."
Derek Sivers: sparse /now page — each item is 1-2 sentences max, and stands alone.

The test for every sentence: *if I removed this, would the reader lose something
meaningful?* If no — cut it.

## Structure for a Minimal Portfolio Page

Based on analysis of Omar Waseem (omarwasm.com) and similar exemplars:

```
[NAME / IDENTITY] — One line. Lowercase, personal, direct.

[THESIS] — One sentence. Who you are at the deepest level. Not your job title.
           Should plant a question in the reader's mind.

[PROOF BLOCK 1 — Research/Work] — 2-3 sentences. Numbers. Specific company names.
                                   Hint at ambition without stating it.

[PROOF BLOCK 2 — Scale/World] — 2-3 sentences. The most unexpected credential.
                                  Tension: how did a researcher end up here?

[NOW + INVITATION] — 1-2 sentences. Where you're going. Soft CTA.
```

Total: 5 paragraphs max. Each one should be readable in under 15 seconds.

## The NDA Rule
Frame the NDA as a status signal, not a limitation.

| ❌ Apologetic | ✅ Confident |
|---|---|
| "I can't say much because of NDA" | "Can't say more — strong NDA." |
| "Due to confidentiality I'm unable..." | "One of the largest business series launches of 2025 on YouTube — can't say more." |

The NDA line proves: you operate in circles that require confidentiality.
That is more powerful than the name of whoever it was.

## Common Mistakes — Ranked by How Often They Appear

1. **Listing instead of storytelling** — bullets of titles/credentials with no context
2. **No tension anywhere** — every claim is positive, smooth, unchallenged
3. **Generic adjectives** — "passionate", "innovative", "results-driven" — mean nothing
4. **Audience blindness** — writing what's impressive to you, not interesting to them
5. **Oversharing** — emotional detail that doesn't serve the narrative
6. **No CTA** — builds engagement then leaves the reader with nowhere to go

---

## Output Format When Using This Skill

Produce 3 versions with different opening hooks:
- **Version A**: Opens with identity
- **Version B**: Opens with the most unexpected fact/number
- **Version C**: Opens with a statement that creates immediate curiosity/tension

For each version:
- 5 paragraphs max
- First-person, conversational
- No adjectives that don't add info ("passionate", "innovative", etc.)
- End with where you're going + soft invitation

Then: recommend which version is strongest and explain why in 2-3 sentences.

---

## E-E-A-T Checklist Before Finalizing

- [ ] Does it feel like a real person talking?
- [ ] Does every paragraph have at least one specific fact or number?
- [ ] Is there at least one moment of tension or honest admission?
- [ ] Is the NDA handled as a feature, not an apology?
- [ ] Does it end with where you're going?
- [ ] Would removing any sentence make the page weaker?
